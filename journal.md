When learning how to make a website with Markdown and GitHub, I explored committing and pushing changes from VS Code to GitHub. This surprised me, as I had very little knowledge of cross-platform coding and editing. I learned a lot by just trying things out and seeing what happened. Markdown is cool; it is simple enough, like English, but easy to understand and memorize. I found it helpful that I could make lists, headings, and links so easily. I also liked that I could see my changes right away. I hope future readers will think about the design, font, graphic, and typography choices I made. Even small choices can change how people feel when they visit a site. My overall aspirations are to create a cool website to host and showcase my work for future job opportunities. I would like to be able to have a portfolio of EVERY SINGLE piece of work I have ever made in University. This would help me remember what I learned and show my progress over time, as well as show improvement for my own personal reference and liking.


While I was creating my prototypes, I learned how to name objects into what they are for proper organization (EX: naming the ellipse 'head' instead of simply ellipse). Nothing is difficult to understand, just takes a decently timely manner to properly organize everything. A good habit I developed was note taking and better pushing messages, which helps me understand my code and for anyone else interested in reading it.
